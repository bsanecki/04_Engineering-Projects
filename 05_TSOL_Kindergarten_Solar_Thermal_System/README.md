# T*SOL Solar Thermal System Design - Kindergarten

## Project Overview

A comparative simulation study of a **solar thermal system for domestic hot water (DHW)** in a kindergarten, designed using **T*SOL 2025 R1**. The project includes two scenarios based on climate data for Olsztyn and Rzeszów.

The study focuses on solar collector configuration, hot water demand, thermal energy production, auxiliary heating requirements, system efficiency and CO₂ emission reductions.

## Scope of Work

- Configuration of a solar thermal system for domestic hot water production.
- Selection and arrangement of solar thermal collectors.
- Definition of collector area, orientation and tilt angle.
- Configuration of a hot water storage tank.
- Setup of auxiliary heating using a gas-fired boiler.
- Definition of DHW demand and climate parameters.
- Simulation of annual solar energy production and system efficiency.
- Analysis of solar contribution to hot water demand.
- Evaluation of auxiliary energy requirements and CO₂ emission reductions.
- Comparison of simulation results between Olsztyn and Rzeszów.

## Technical Notes

Both scenarios use 10 Viessmann Vitosol 200-F Typ XL10 solar collectors, with a total gross reference area of 100.5 m² and an active area of 93.3 m². The system also includes a 7.5 m³ DHW storage tank and auxiliary heating provided by a Viessmann Vitoradial 300-T boiler (313 kW).

The reports were generated using **T*SOL 2025 R1 Non-commercial edition**. The simulations use local climate data for Olsztyn and Rzeszów.

The reports contain an input inconsistency: the DHW consumption profile is labelled as a multifamily residential building rather than a kindergarten. Additionally, the specified average daily hot water consumption differs significantly between the scenarios. These limitations should be considered when comparing the results.

All presented values are software simulation outputs based on the assumptions and input parameters documented in the individual PDF reports. Actual system performance may differ depending on weather conditions, hot water demand and installation-specific factors.
